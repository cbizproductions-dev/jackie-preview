What the research says

Stress lives in the body. So does relief.

Modern research keeps catching up with what Eastern medicine has practiced for centuries. Here's what the evidence shows about massage, acupuncture and stress.

83%

Of 18 massage studies in a 2024 systematic review, 15 found a significant drop in anxiety. Researchers concluded manual therapies "consistently decrease anxiety intensity."

West & Huzij, Journal of Osteopathic Medicine, 2024 · 34 studies reviewed
−31%

Average drop in the stress hormone cortisol after massage, with serotonin up 28% and dopamine up 31%.

Field et al., Int. Journal of Neuroscience, 2005 · review of 30+ studies
20,827

Patients across 39 trials showed acupuncture outperformed both sham and no acupuncture for chronic pain, and the effect can't be explained by placebo alone.

Vickers et al., The Journal of Pain, 2018
~85%

Of acupuncture's pain-relief effect was still present a full year after treatment ended.

Same meta-analysis: effect declined about 15% at 12 months
400%

Increase in surface microcirculation in the minutes after gua sha, with every participant reporting less pain.

Nielsen et al., Explore, 2007 · small pilot study (11 adults)
800

Adults 65 and older with chronic low back pain took part in a 2025 NIH-funded trial. Acupuncture groups had less pain-related disability at 6 and 12 months than usual care, plus fewer anxiety symptoms.

BackInAction trial, JAMA Network Open, Sept 2025
83%

Of U.S. adults stressed by societal division reported at least one physical stress symptom in the past month, like anxiety, fatigue or headaches.

APA Stress in America™, Nov 2025
